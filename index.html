About

About

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Political Science at the University of Pennsylvania, where I am also completing a dual M.A. in Statistics. My research sits at the intersection of international relations and political methodology: I use computational text analysis and machine learning to study how authoritarian governments project influence, how media frame political conflict, and how to measure civic space in developing democracies.

Before Penn, I earned an M.A. in Economics and Political Science from Duke University and an M.A. in Economics and International Relations from the University of St Andrews. From 2020 to 2022 I worked on the Machine Learning for Peace project at DEVLAB@Duke, ultimately as Research Manager. I currently serve as an editorial assistant at Political Analysis, and my job market paper received the Penn Grad Talks Social Science prize in 2026. I am on the academic job market for 2026–2027.

Research

Research

  • i. International relationsAuthoritarian foreign influence, the politics of news media coverage of conflict, and how states signal during crises.
  • ii. Computational text analysisBuilding large corpora of domestic news media and applying transformer models to measure political phenomena — particularly civic space in developing democracies.
  • iii. Political methodologyNew approaches to measurement, decomposition, and forecasting, including a zero-loss decomposition of the 2024 U.S. electorate.
